About

I'm an independent security researcher based in Indonesia. My work is bug bounty hunting in the literal sense: pick a program, read its code and traffic until something doesn't add up, prove it with a working proof of concept, and file a report a triager can act on without asking follow-up questions.

Most of my time goes to self-hosted programs that never appear on the big platforms — found through dorks, public program lists, and watchers I run around the clock. The other half is Web3: protocol reviews where one wrong assumption in a contract is worth more than a hundred scanner findings.

  • R-01

    Proof or it didn't happenEvery claim ships with a runnable PoC — a canary hit, a trace, a recording.

  • R-02

    Scope first, alwaysPermission and rules of engagement before the first request is sent.

  • R-03

    Honest severityIf the real impact is a DoS, the report says DoS — not account takeover.

  • R-04

    One hypothesis at a timeGate-based validation: confirmed, falsified, or inconclusive. No spray and pray.

How I work

step_01

Recon

Map the program, the scope, and the stack. Watchers surface new targets; I pick the ones with thin coverage.

step_02

Hypothesis

One concrete claim about one component, written down before any testing starts.

step_03

Validation

Two-account proof, canary callbacks, measured impact. Confirmed, falsified, or inconclusive — all three are results.

step_04

Report

Reproduction steps, PoC attached, severity argued from evidence. Then I track the thread until it closes.

Server-side request forgery

Unauthenticated endpoints that fetch what you tell them to. Canary-confirmed, with the honest limits of the port oracle stated in the report.SSRF · canary · port oracle · metadata

Access control & session flaws

Registration and session flows where one account quietly becomes another. Proven with two accounts, never with speculation.IDOR · auth bypass · session takeover
